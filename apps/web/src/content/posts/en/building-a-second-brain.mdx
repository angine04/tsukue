We are not meant to keep everything in our heads. Our minds are for
thinking, not storing. Yet we are taught to memorise, retain, and
remember — until our thoughts become cluttered rooms with no clear
shelves.

A second brain is a system for capturing, organising, and connecting
what matters. It is not just a digital archive. It is a place where
ideas can breathe, evolve, and reveal their true value over time.

## Capture is cheap, retrieval is not

The mistake is treating collection as the work. Saving an article feels
like progress and is almost never progress.

What earns its place is the note you write _about_ the thing, in your
own words, attached to a problem you currently have.

## Connections beat categories

Folders assume you know what something is for. You usually don't yet.

A link between two half-formed ideas is worth more than either one
filed correctly, because the link is the part you could not have
looked up.

## Let it stay small

A second brain that grows without pruning is a junk drawer with better
typography. Delete more than you add, and the system keeps paying for
itself.
