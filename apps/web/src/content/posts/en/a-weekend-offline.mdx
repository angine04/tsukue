I left the phone in a drawer from Friday evening to Sunday morning
and kept a paper note of every time I reached for it.

The count was thirty-one on the first day and nine on the second. Not
because I have unusual self-control, but because the reaching turned out
to be a habit with no object — a stutter, not a want.

## What came back

The first thing was boredom, which is unpleasant for about twenty
minutes and then becomes something else. The second was length. A
conversation lasted an hour because neither of us had anywhere else to
be.

The third was the discovery that almost nothing I missed had been
addressed to me specifically. It was a tide, not a message.

## Not a manifesto

I went back online on Monday. I am not proposing anyone live like this
permanently, and a weekend is too short to prove anything about a life.

But it is long enough to notice that the default is a choice someone
made, and that it is reversible.
